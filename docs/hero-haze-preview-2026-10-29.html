How Photo smoke works. Two extra copies of the photo sit over the smoke, cut out by a soft mask that stops before his shoulder. One is sharp, one is blurred. They cross-fade on 15 and 21 second loops while moving a few percent of the frame and growing or shrinking slightly. Frozen shows the still photo.

Smoke blob and headline shading. The blob is an irregular grey shape pushed around by animated noise, so its edges curl and tear as it drifts. The headline text is filled with slow grey-to-white noise plus white wisps, clipped to the letters. T and V keep their test-card colors. Both have checkboxes so you can compare. Cost: the blob re-draws its filter every frame, so it would be desktop-only.

Behind the text. A mask cuts the haze to about a third where the headline sits, so the white title and green subtitle keep their contrast. Strength scales the whole layer.

On phones and tablets. The live site freezes its haze at 1024px and under to protect the mobile Lighthouse score. The Frozen button shows that resting frame. In the real build the wider haze would keep that rule and turn off under reduced motion.
