# Doug Rosenberg — *Better Than TV*: Review Roundup

Compiled October 23, 2026.

## About the album

- **Artist:** Doug Rosenberg (saxophone, composition)
- **Released:** October 17, 2013 (digital); CD issued 2014 on Proteus Ent.
- **Length:** 8 tracks, about 57 minutes
- **Personnel (per Chicagoist):** Rob Clearfield (piano), Matt Ulery (bass), Makaya McCraven (drums); Marquis Hill (trumpet) on the title track
- **Tracks:** Which Ball May Fall · Better Than TV · Dirty Basement Jazz · Mato Grosso · The Comeback · A Higher Standard · Snove · Forged Stability

## Reviews found

### Chicagoist — Chris Bentley, December 9, 2013

The one full, dedicated review I could locate. Headline: "Stop Channel Surfing, Chicago Saxophonist Doug Rosenberg's Debut 'Better Than TV' Delivers."

Summary (paraphrased):

- Bentley opens with "The Comeback," which he describes as free-jazz solos wrapped in tight, up-tempo funk, ranging from delicate to huge. He says the ideas sound like they're bursting out, yet nothing feels rushed.
- The title track is called a topsy-turvy post-bop tune in 5/4, with clever rhythmic shifts from the bass and drums keeping listeners engaged without being heavy-handed.
- "Mato Grosso" is praised for samba/bossa-style syncopation and voicings, combined with 6/4 time and an asymmetrical structure.
- He notes the band moves easily across styles and eras. Despite Rosenberg's Eastern Blok background, the album avoids obvious Balkan touches, though Bentley hears a soulfulness that might come from that experience.
- The sidemen are singled out: Clearfield's shimmering chords on "Forged Stability," and Ulery's rhythmic counterpoint on "Snove" and "A Higher Standard."
- Verdict: a distinctive composer and full-bodied tone, backed by a poised young all-star group. The closing line says Rosenberg may have "put down the remote" and made something better than TV.

Source: <https://chicagoist.com/2013/12/09/stop_channel_surfing_-_chicago_saxo.php>

## A caveat on the Chicago Tribune quote

Rosenberg's bio and label copy credit the Chicago Tribune with praising his "fearlessness of spirit" and "robustness of tone." I could not find the original Tribune article, so I can't confirm it is a review of this album rather than of his live playing. A pull request on the GitHub repo for dougrosenberg.com also notes that no source for that quote had been located. Treat it as an unverified attribution until the article turns up.

Watch out for name collisions in searches. All About Jazz's "Better Than TV" page is for a different, UK-based band, and The Jazz Session's April 2014 episode is with saxophonist Ed Rosenberg III, not Doug.
